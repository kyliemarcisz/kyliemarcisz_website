Princeton 3D3A Lab
AI Head-Tracking · Anechoic Research

Selected as 1 of 4 students from 400+ applicants for the Princeton Laboratory Learning Program. I worked alongside Prof. Edgar Choueiri evaluating AI-integrated head-tracking algorithms inside an anechoic chamber — and integrated those findings directly into BACCH's Optimal Room Correction prototype.

Key insight: Real-time head-tracking dramatically improves the perceived stability of a virtual sound source — the brain expects audio to stay fixed in space even as you move.

CNMAT Research
Planar Loudspeaker Arrays · Hearing Health & Sustainability
Fall 2026 → Present

This semester I joined CNMAT as a researcher studying whether large planar loudspeaker arrays — built with Marziani's technology — can produce a more spatially uniform sound field than conventional live-event systems, reducing localized high-SPL exposure, electrical consumption, equipment, and deployment labor while holding perceived loudness, clarity, and intelligibility constant. Cal Hacks 13.0 at the Palace of Fine Arts is the project's first real-world test: measuring SPL, LAeq, and intelligibility across the venue against a conventional system, while tracking power draw, equipment weight, and labor-hours side by side — then modeling both against a 3D acoustic digital twin of the venue built in Unreal Engine.

Core question: can listeners receive less acoustic exposure without perceiving the experience as quieter, and can that same experience be delivered with less power, equipment, and labor? A new metric — resources per listener (kWh, equipment weight, and labor-hours per 1,000 attendee-hours) — aims to make that comparison concrete.
